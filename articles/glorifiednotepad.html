Glorified Notepad

This is the first issue to be locally hosted within the website. If there are any errors or formatting problems, please send an email to help@shreddedpaper.ca and I'll be right on it.

It’s 1946, and America’s first breakthrough in data-storage and calculation has finally been finished. Taking up the at the time ridiculous size of an 1800sqft building, ENIAC, or the Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer, is up and running. According to various sources, it cost around $400,000 usd, or around $7,000,000 usd adjusting for inflation combined with thirty months of around the clock labor to assemble and test. In the papers ENIAC was touted to be used for engineering purposes, nuclear physics that could aid US intelligence, and could theoretically solve mathematical problems thousands of times faster than the average person. After it was made operational, it was handed over to the army. They utilized it for a few weeks and fed it ballistic missile data in an attempt to find the most efficient distance to launch from. Like a child with a new toy, it was neglected, used as an encryption device to store launch codes. It remained a glorified notepad until its 1955 decommission.

Jumping forward into 1963, IBM unveils the “IBM Federal Systems Division’s Data Processing Center”, a project that has minimal coverage. Consider it an archaic data-warehouse, the place lined with top of the notch computer devices dedicated to multitasking for the federal government's needs. Transistors were introduced to replace the bulky vacuum tubes, and the world’s first “supercomputer” popped up, the CDC6600 in 1964. While Cray’s CDC6600 was actually partially produced as a commercial product marketed towards larger companies, the original one was purchased by the Army. Both of these products remained “in use” for around 5 years, but yet again these 5 years were mostly to store launch codes or calculate missile data. The CDC6600 would be considered slightly more than just a glorified notepad though, as a large (for the era) number of these products were put to actual use, and most famously disproved Euler's sum of powers conjecture in 1966 using direct numerical search.

This pattern continues, a new groundbreaking system is released, and the army buys it. This would loop until consumer products became more digitized and it was now more profitable to sell to consumers considering the increasingly cheap value of electronic parts. While there isn’t a set year for this shift, I’d consider it around the late 70s, which is when the personal computers became on par with what was considered “powerful” for the era. Fueled by increasing profitability and people blindly throwing money into certain groups, the industry boomed, and wouldn’t see much innovation past “how many headphone jacks can be added until it’s no longer a ‘feature’”, that is until the cloud-computing era post “.com” bubble. IDCs (Internet Data Centers) were built at a violently accelerating rate because companies needed fast internet connectivity and non-stop operation to “establish” themselves in the industry. These IDCs were complete energy-whores, and raised a variety of concerns due to the expensive cost of cooling. During the crisis these companies got instantly liquidated, and had to either merge with existing groups or go bankrupt, both resulting in many of these IDCs being destined for decommission. The federal government acquired a vast number of these facilities. According to the OMRCS, there were around 300 federal data centers in 1998, and in 2010 this had grown to around 2000 federal facilities across the USA. The chief information officer for the government in 2010, Vivek Kundra, opened up to the press about the government deciding to close 800 of their centers, citing rising costs. Their glorified notepads being a bit too expensive, they planned to close a few hundred per year. Kundra shed more light on the situation, saying “The closings are determined by technology managers in the federal agencies. Data centers are not huge employers”. Remember; data centers are not huge employers.

In early 2012, not a single data center had been closed. In fact, they had purchased another.

These centers remained open, but the environmental impact faded from the public eyes. Lay low until people forget, a dead-possum type method. Jumping all the way until the present day, and we’re seeing a similar series of patterns seen in the “.com” bubble of on yonder. There are currently 4288 commercial data centers in the USA, this does not include the 2400 federal centers. Over 70% of the commercial centers were built in the last 3 years alone. The culprit is obvious, the rising costs of “AI”. Not the exact same, but similarities between the events. In these past few years and possibly continuing further, hundreds of billions of dollars will be sold, exchanged, tracked, and changed hands. But the customers have changed. The American government has no longer been purchasing and holding onto these glorified notepads. No, the government is absolutely fucking broke, and with the current administration about as intelligent as a room of babies at a beer-drinking contest, they gave the keys to the green-light to the most trust-worthy group in the world, billionaires.

Profiteers do what they’re addicted to, make money. While the consumer market has been a great driver for profits and gains, it’s time to cut the human element. In April of 2026, one of Nevada’s largest commercial entities for energy, NVEng, made a fucking instagram announcement saying that they will be ceasing all commercial energy operations to route all their sources to all customers by the end of 2026. Customers feeling stranded have been forced to switch to the competitors, which have upped their rates due to the massive influx of clients. Ram prices spiked because Micron swapped to the “AI Market”. AI slop is being injected into every single thing you use, your browser, your gpu, your social media feed, all to prop up the “usage” to push numbers and attract more money and investments. Luckily, the American government is busy making AI images of trump being depicted as god to actually fucking doing anything. So at least they can’t actively make shit worse.

Do you really want to be the one to put even more into the hands of the profiteers?

- The Chairman
