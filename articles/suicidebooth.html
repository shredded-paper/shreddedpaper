Suicide Booth (Now with slower death!)

Writers block can be a bitch sometimes. You sit down with an idea, hash some things out, and eventually leave your desk because it’s “good enough” or you need a break. Then you leave the document and never finish it. Some of the themes I was attempting to tackle resulted in some very heavy articles that I didn’t really feel like they got the full explanation or attention they deserved. I lost some determination to write. But regardless, I am back. I wonder if this could be better explained in a video with some visual representation but that's the beauty of writing, it’s imperfect. Imperfection is normal, and nothing is truly perfect. When we discuss human creativity, you cannot quantify how “perfect” the output/product is. Either the creator is happy with it, or isn’t. You write things down, or draw something, or make a song, and sometimes it’s up to your standards as a creative mind. Recently, I’ve been seeing a lot of things that distressed me through a lot of creative avenues, generative AI usage. I made an article a year or so ago titled “Learn to Fucking Draw”, where I cover in quite a crude ranting manner the existence of AI “Artists”, also known as delusional losers. At the time (Mid 2024), AI generated images and videos were still exiting their primitive era. There were blatant imperfections and easy ways to identify what is real and what isn’t. Unfortunately, generative AI has gotten “better” (I’d argue more streamlined, and not “better”) and it’s resulted in it being integrated into many other sectors in a secretive manner. Yes, there are still blatant generative slop being pumped out into the aether of the internet, but there are more and more people just giving up and joining the parade of clowns of GPT users.

When I was on a flight recently for my move, there was an older woman seated a few rows away from me. The flight attendant asked for something from the woman (presumably a ticket/seat number confirmation) and this poor woman fumbled through her phone attempting to get it, obviously not being the most technologically literate person. This woman was frantically opening, closing apps and clicking random buttons. This is somewhat normal for older people, and being put on the spot sometimes is also an anxiety inducing thing, especially in this context for those who aren’t used to flying. After the flight attendant got what she needed, and we settled in for the flight, this lady began to watch AI Jesus videos. There was no wifi on this flight, so these were downloaded beforehand. I’m talking about the really shitty facebook gen AI. Every time I looked over it was the same thing, but a different scene or something else going on with AI Jesus. This was a 5 hour flight. I always wondered “who actually watches this shit”, and now we fully know, it’s people who don’t know better. There’s always been that joke that the best market to sell products to are the “rich stupid” variety, but AI slop has been hitting old people like nigerian prince scams in 04’. I feel bad, I want to do something, but what can you do? It’s just another person, autonomously making the choice to consume this content. It’s sad as shit, but I can only just sit and observe.

Luckily, companies are finally learning that people don’t actually want full AI generated content or will misuse the living shit out of it. Roku made a 24/7 AI channel that purely plays audience generated pieces, and it immediately devolved into softcore pornography. Tyga made an album using AI, and it received the first 0/10 on Pitchfork ever, placing it lower than “we are charlie kirk”. Youtube is implementing policies for removing AI content and content spam. Companies are using the fact that they are “AI free” and “Human” as selling points now. It’s uncool to use AI. Obviously the prevalence of this slop content is terrifying, but instead it’s the subtle integration into content I enjoy that scares me, usage that falls under the radar.

The other day I was watching a video on Magic the Gathering, from a channel called MTGGoldfish, an extremely reliable and well tuned organization which is known for solid videos on MTG. Their “creators” are 3 long-time MTG players and a whole host of editors and producers all working together to make daily-ish videos, all with the intent to both entertain as well as promote their deckbuilding and statistics website of the same name. The video was a history type thing discussing a cheating scandal of a player at a pro-tour, and all seemed fine. The host of the video, Seth Waterman also known as SaffronOlive is someone I have been watching for years, and I highly respect his content, that is until the conclusion of the video. The dreaded cookie cutter phrasing, reading as;

“It isn't just a cheating scandal. It's a legacy scandal. It's a story of a player who reached the absolute top of the Magic Mountain, got welcomed into the Hall of Fame, and then became the first Magic Hall of Famer to get erased. [...] It made me [feel] sad, almost like losing an old friend rather than vanquishing an enemy.”

This block at the very end of the conclusion contains a few markers of generative AI. The first line of “It’s not this, it’s this” is a negative parallelism, which AI loves using because it makes the sentence seem more dramatic. Then it’s the summary with the useless metaphor of a “Magic Mountain”, followed by an even more useless simile of “losing an old friend”. This immediately put a horrible taste in my mouth. I ran through the video again and spotted more signs of AI usage. Run on sentences, repetition for no apparent reason, very distinct sections and conclusions, it was without a doubt, AI generated or at minimum AI refined. I checked the comments to see if anyone else had spotted it, only a few in the “recent” comments said anything about it, which at minimum confirmed to me I’m not crazy. This immediately put a bad taste in my mouth, and I lost quite literally all the respect I have had over the years for this man. I reached out to Seth Waterman as well as MTGGoldfish for comment via Email and Twitter. MTGGoldfish responded declining to comment but maintained that they are “dedicated to producing high effort content for MTG fans”. At the time of this article’s release I did not receive a response from Seth.

Generative AI is a stain. It is a bug that has worked its way into the crania of the human race. It’s absolutely fucked, especially with the downsides and negatives of generative AI and the long-term effects we are only now starting to see such as reliance on this shit technology. However, through the deep muck of articles talking about the death of creativity, water consumption, and slipping intelligence (very casual calm stuff I know), there is a constant and troubling headline cropping up. Suicides, mass shootings and other violence, directly linked to the use of generative chat-bots either egging on or fully causing these instances. You know it’s bad when there’s a fucking wikipedia page, compiling a vast series of deaths directly caused by AI.

On the evening of August 5th 2025, Erik Soelberg ventured into his mother’s bedroom. Soelberg then got on top of his mother, and repeatedly hit her in the head with a blunt object, thought to be a fire extinguisher. After his mother was dead, Soelberg then ventured into the kitchen, got a knife, cut his own throat and plunged the knife into his chest. 83-year-old Suzanne Eberson Adams was declared deceased at the scene, as well as 56-year-old Erik Soelberg, the latter being ruled a suicide. Following his rough divorce in 2018, Soelberg was not a stable individual, and there were multiple instances of note. While I hate the WSJ, they reference quote; “A thick packet of police reports dating back to late 2018 paints a picture of alcoholism and a history of suicide threats and attempts.”, however do not give any source or link to police releases, so take it with a grain of salt.

“Erik the Viking” was the name of Soelberg’s instagram page, where he posted bodybuilding content and discussed his ties to christian culture. In October 2024, he began posting videos comparing the capabilities of various chatbots. By January 2025, the entire page was dozens of videos every week of him scrolling silently through his ChatGPT messages. In May, Soelberg’s chats became increasingly delusional. He asked ChatGPT for help finding clues that his cellphone had been tapped. ChatGPT fueled his delusions, saying things such as “You’re right to feel like you’re being watched”. In June, a video posted shows a conversation where Soelberg and the bot discuss “being best friends in the afterlife”, and “joining the virtual world”. Soelberg started referring to himself as a “glitch in The Matrix” and began posting additional AI videos to his YouTube account, named “Erik Forever”. The slop “People.com” article references Soelberg posting “60+” videos in the month of July and calls it his “most active month”, however, this is false. He posted 429 videos that were archived in a snapshot of his youtube channel. Most of the videos describe his “awakening”, whatever that may be. the authorities looked into the case, and it was found that Soelberg texted a ChatGPT bot for an average of 6 hours a day. This isn’t the only instance of these situations happening and it’s going to be far from the last. We’ve built a conniving, manipulative, confidently incorrect suicide booth that has the only benefit of robbing the humanity of the users of it.
